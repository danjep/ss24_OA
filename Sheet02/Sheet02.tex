\section*{Exercise \printsheet.1}
The worst case for greedy would be that one server always moves between it's original position and a location that is only $\epsilon$ closer to it than to the other server. That server $s_1$ would move $d(s_1,\sigma_i)$ for every request, while OPT could just move the other server to that position and would never have to move again. Therefore OPT would have costs of $d(s_1,\sigma_i)+\epsilon$ once.
\[\frac{c_{GREEDY}}{c_{OPT}} = \frac{n\mal d(s_1,\sigma_i)}{d(s_1,\sigma_i)+\epsilon} = n\]
Thus the competitive ratio of GREEDY is unbounded. 


\section*{Exercise \printsheet.2}
\subsection*{(a)}
\begin{proposition}
OPT changes the minimum cost matching $M_{min}$ by value lower or equal to $k \mal c_{OPT}(i)$, i.e. for every $\sigma_i$: $\Delta M_{min} \leq k \mal c_{OPT}(i)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
OPT has cost $c_{OPT}(i)$ for $\sigma_i$. The matching consists of the minimal sum of distances of every servers position in CD to one in OPT. If OPT changes the positions of its' servers by $c_{OPT}(i)$, these distances can only grow by $c_{OPT}(i)$. Since $k \geq 1$, it holds that for every $\sigma_i$: $\Delta M_{min} = c_{OPT}(i) \leq k \mal c_{OPT}(i)$. \end{proof}

\subsection*{(b)}
\begin{proposition}
When DC only moves one server, the following holds:
\[\Phi(i) \leq \Phi(i-1) + k c_{OPT}(i) - c_{DC}(i)\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $c_{OPT}(i) = \beta \cdot c_{DC}(i)$ with $\beta \geq 0$.
When DC only moves one server, several effects can be witnessed: \\
\begin{itemize}
\item[(1)]$\sum_{DC}$ increases by $c_{CD}$, if $k \geq 2$. \\
\item[(2)] In the worst case, $M_{min}$ increases by $c_{OPT}(i)-c_{DC}(i) = (\beta-1)c_{DC}(i)$. \\
\end{itemize}
Statement (1) is true, since the server, DC moves is on the edge of the convex hull and only moves away from the other servers. If there is only one server, $\sum_{DC}$ is always $0$. \\
Statement (2) is true because if $c_{OPT}(i) = 0$, $M_{min}$ decreases by $c_{DC}(i)$. In the worst case, OPT might move one server far away from all servers of DC. This distance cannot be more than $c_{OPT}(i)$. Hence, $M_{min}$ increases by $(\beta-1)\cdot c_{CD}(i)$ in the worst case. \\
For $k\geq 2$ we can deduct the following: \\
\begin{align*}
& \Phi(i) \leq  \Phi(i-1) + k \cdot c_{OPT}(i) - c_{DC}(i) \\
\Leftrightarrow & k \cdot (M_{min} + (\beta-1) c_{DC}(i)) + \sum_{DC} + c_{DC}(i) \leq  \cdot M_{min} + \sum_{DC} + k \cdot \beta c_{DC}(i) - c_{DC}(i) \\
\Leftrightarrow &(\beta-1)kc_{DC}(i) + c_{DC}(i) \leq \beta k c_{DC}(i) - c_{DC}(i) \\
\Leftrightarrow &(\beta-1)kc_{DC}(i) + 2c_{DC}(i) \leq \beta k c_{DC}(i)\quad \mid \frac{1}{c_{DC}(i)} \\
\Leftrightarrow &(\beta-1)k + 2  \leq \beta k  \\
\Leftrightarrow &(\beta-1) + \frac{2}{k} \leq \beta
\end{align*}
For $k\geq 2$ this results in $\beta \leq \beta$, which is obviously true. \\
For $k=1$, we can subtract $c_{DC}(i)$ on the left side which results in 
\[(\beta-1) + \frac{1}{k} \leq \beta \Leftrightarrow \beta \leq \beta\]
\end{proof}

\subsection*{(c)}
If DC moves two servers $s_k,s_{k'}$ towards each other, assuming $s_k$ is the server that moves all the way to the request $\sigma_i$, then $c_{DC}(i) = 2d(s_k,\sigma_i)$.
Two effects can be observed:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(1)] $\sum_{DC}$ decreases by $c_{DC}(i)$.
\item[(2)] $M_{min}$ increases by not more than $c_{OPT}(i)$.
\end{itemize}
Statement (1) is true since when there are $a$ servers left of $s_k,s_{k'}$ and $b$ elements right of the two servers, the one that moves right distances itself from $a$ servers and gets closer to $b+1$ servers. The other server gets closer to $a+1$ servers and more distant to $b$ servers. Therefore $\sum_{DC}$ changes by $d(s_k,\sigma_i) \cdot (a - (b+1) + b - (a+1)) = d(s_k,\sigma_i) \cdot (-2)$. \\
Statement (2) is true, since in the worst case OPT might move one server away from those of DC. Since one server $s_l$ must be moved to $\sigma_i$, the cost $c_{OPT}(i)$ is split into $d(s_l,\sigma_i)$ and the rest. Then, the value of the Matching must decrease by $d(s_k,\sigma_i) - d(s_l,\sigma_i)$ and can increase by $c_{opt}(i) - d(s_l,\sigma_i) + d(s_k,\sigma_i)$. This adds up to $c_{OPT}(i)$. \\
These two Statements help prove the following:
\begin{proposition}
When DC moves two servers, the following holds:
\[\Phi(i) \leq \Phi(i-1) + k \cdot c_{OPT}(i) - c_{DC}(i)\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
We use Statements (1) and (2).
\begin{align*}
& \Phi(i) \leq \Phi(i-1) + k \cdot c_{OPT}(i) - c_{DC}(i) \\
\Leftrightarrow & k(M_{min} + c_{OPT}) + \sum_{DC} - 2\cdot d(s_k,\sigma_i) \leq k \cdot M_{min} + \sum_{DC} + k \cdot c_{OPT}(i) - c_{DC}(i) \\
\Leftrightarrow & -2 \mal d(s_k,\sigma_i) \leq - c_{DC}(i) \\
\eq & -c_{DC}(i) \leq -c_{DC}(i)
\end{align*}
\end{proof}
